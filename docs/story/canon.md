# Playable canon — Leaving Home and Prison Trains

The old Disks A–D story has been replaced. Its rescues, time-travel railway, registry, receipts and missing-father plot are not part of this campaign. The full planned story, including the separate final chapter in the god's domain, is in [grand-outline.md](grand-outline.md). Chapters Three–Seven are unimplemented author knowledge, not facts the party already knows.

- Nell is seventeen, a railway apprentice at Larkspur. Her older brother Tomas, twenty-four, has returned from the capital. They have promised to see the sea together. Nell carries Ansel's oil lantern.
- Larkspur is holding a festival. The empire credits the Benefactor with crystal lighting, plentiful harvests, healing and its engines. The optional festival-lantern interaction records Nell learning the patron's name and emblem (`blessingRead`). The public festival blessing is not proof of the bargain's terms.
- Stationmaster Ansel is Nell's mentor. Iona supplies the station. Orrin has built a coal-fired engine independent of imperial crystal signals.
- Captain Alban arrives to arrest Tomas for stealing a crystal from the palace engine. Ansel intervenes and is fatally wounded. Tomas drops the crystal during the struggle. A pale fleck catches in Nell's lantern glass. Its handle hums and feels cold. Neither the scene nor the journal establishes what the crystal can do; no divine voice speaks.
- Tomas is taken aboard the eastbound prison coach. His last instruction is to find Orrin and use the old branch. Ansel dies in Nell's arms after directing her west. `raidSeen` records the entire scene only when it completes or is skipped. A return visit offers Ansel's cap and Iona's acknowledgment, never a living Ansel.
- Nell defeats two pursuit hounds in Larkspur square (`escapeWon`), opening the western road. The battle uses the station backdrop. This is the only solo battle.
- Orrin hears what happened, supports Nell and joins deliberately (`orrinJoined`). His engine can use the Rillbridge branch to reach the eastern pass. No fuse or repair errands gate recruitment or departure.
- An optional workshop encounter (`workshopWon`) rewards the brass lantern reflector. It is not needed to proceed. Neither rewards nor supplies can be collected repeatedly.
- Rillbridge has stopped. Its people, smoke and water are motionless. The imperial collector is still running at the eastern carriage. The lantern hums more strongly here, but the party does not explain why they can move or attribute time powers to the crystal. `villageSeen` records this arrival.
- Through the collector's signal horn, Alban calls the operation a repayment. The party defeats the collector guard (`collectorDefeated`), then physically opens the casing in the release scene (`villageFreed`). The villagers wake. Tilda has lost three days and her hands hurt; Elspeth cannot lift her register. The party stopped further taking, but has not restored what was already taken.
- Tilda directs them to the eastern pass, where Tomas's coach went. Tilda and Elspeth remain in the village and acknowledge being freed on repeat visits. The north road opens only after the rescue.
- Alban holds the pass. The optional mural shows harvesters carrying sheaves toward a damaged sun carving. Orrin observes that it predates the imperial track; nobody explains the god's realm yet.
- The shelter offers free rest and two bandages once. It does not advance a countdown. The captain uses a blast attack; orange steam outlets discharge after each combat round. Movement, ally damage, cover and previews retain the engine's existing rules.
- Defeating Alban (`captainDefeated`) genuinely opens the crossing. He retreats alive. In the departure scene he says Tomas is at the Iron Coast prison-train exchange and warns that the exchange is under military guard. Nell and Orrin choose to follow in their own engine.
- The chapter ends with Nell and Orrin leaving together, without a palace cutaway, divine speech or a revealed source for the lantern's hum. The completed departure sets `chapterOneComplete`, boundary `chapter-two:iron-coast`, and the `departure` journal entry together.

Save version 7 identifies campaign `imperial-debt` and stores a shared equipment bag with individual loadouts. Version-6 saves gain their existing party as the active formation without changing progress. Version-5 saves migrate their old gear into equipped copies without changing progress. Previous-story saves can be exported but are explicitly rejected for play. The old campaign's content and flags must not be used to infer new events. Cutscenes commit at completion; interruptions replay from the preceding checkpoint, and skipping invokes the same completion callback. Exploration and combat are separate from temporary cutscene actors.


## Chapter Two — Prison Trains

- The explicit `chapter-two:iron-coast` boundary opens the chapter. All three regional maps load before it is consumed; failure preserves the complete departure. In a source checkpoint without the coast art pack, Begin Chapter Two is absent. Installed artwork enables the entry automatically.
- The arrival shows the harbor and barred coach before introducing Kit. He saw Tomas at the exchange transfer window. Kit is a resistance courier helping the waiting families open the train. His introduction commits `kitJoined` only at completion and adds him as the third active fighter.
- The player chooses the signal walk (two crows and a hound) or loading gate (an armored guardian and two hounds). The choice is explained before commitment and saved as `raidPlanned` plus `raidDiversion`. Both routes have different battle layouts, pay one shared `trainRaidWon` reward, and add a courier sight to the bag for manual fitting.
- Petra is guarding the coach and recognizes the lantern from Alban’s report. She calls for its surrender. Kit and Orrin move a wagon or loading crane across her route; the party fights through the guard machines. No divine voice speaks. The lantern merely hums against Nell’s hand.
- Opening the coach frees its prisoners (`prisonersFreed`). Celia helps them down. She treated Tomas at the transfer window before his removal and received his transfer slip: palace engine works in the capital, personal custody by imperial order. His added note says the collectors feed the engines and he saw houses go dark. This establishes a practical connection, not the full divine bargain.
- The dispatch case supplies a current medical-train clearance. Kit gets briefly flustered when Nell thanks him, then returns to opening the doors. The moment has no reciprocal implication or later payoff.
- The prisoners go to the railway ward. The official hospital refused to admit people without release papers; Celia opened this ward instead. She joins to accompany her patients and guide the party to the capital’s medical siding (`celiaJoined`, `wardSeen`). With four travelers, she initially enters reserve. Equipment selects up to three active fighters, with Nell always leading. Everyone retains HP, focus and individual equipment and shares campaign experience. Reserves receive normal victory recovery and level growth. Free rest restores everyone. Bandages cost eight marks at the ward.
- Kit directs the party to the medical coaches and waiting mining families at Cinderbank. Alban has ordered the settlement collected to supply the capital’s engines. Petra objects to abandoning the promised evacuation. Alban cites the emperor’s superseding order and tells her to recover the fragment (`settlementSeen`).
- The party defeats the coal-shed collector guard (`coastCollectorWon`) and opens the casing (`evacuationReady`). Families can move again and board the medical train. As at Rillbridge, this does not restore days already taken. Petra changes the points onto the capital line and leaves to bring her soldiers away from the west gate. She helps at a concrete cost but neither joins nor declares a complete change of allegiance.
- Alban tries to retake the prisoners and fragment at the far crossing. The party defeats him (`recaptureWon`); he retreats alive. This is a fresh rear-guard fight with a crow, hound and stronger captain, rather than a reversal of the Chapter One victory.
- Orrin couples his coal engine to the medical coaches. The prisoners and Cinderbank families escape. Kit and Celia deliberately continue with Nell and Orrin. Tomas is alive at the palace engine works, and the clearance gives them a usable way into the capital. The completion commits `chapterTwoComplete`, journal `capitalDeparture`, and `chapter-three:capital` together. No palace or final-god cutaway follows.

The emperor’s first direct scene is reserved for the capital chapter. His order has visible consequences here. The complete bargain, the conduit and the Benefactor’s separate domain remain later discoveries. The Chapter Three rescue must succeed permanently and bring Tomas back alive.
