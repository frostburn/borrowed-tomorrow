# Canon — through Disk D

This is the authored story source of truth. Update it after every chapter. Dialogue, journal entries, and recorded player flags outrank an unimplemented outline.

- The present-day hub is Larkspur station. Nell is its porter. An unscheduled train arrives at 18:43, bearing the old Rillbridge destination board.
- Rillbridge and its branch line were lost in a flood forty years before Nell's day. The public memorial notice lists no survivors.
- Mira remembers a twenty-minute journey. She boarded with her father; he stepped off to ask about a delay, and the train left without him. Her ticket bears 14 October in the flood year. Its punch mark is fresh.
- Mira finds a receiving copy on her train: **14 October, 00:17; thirty-one received, none substituted**, bearing Nell’s signature. At the opening Nell has not written it. The inspector demands it on the platform and warns her not to answer the western relay. This is a new-opening discovery (`aReceiptSeen`), not knowledge retroactively granted to old saves.
- The locomotive's serial number belongs to a scrapped engine. Its axle is warm and freshly oiled.
- Orrin repairs the yard relay with a recovered fuse. Its stamped date is tomorrow. The relay answers with the old Rillbridge call: two short rings, one long. It prints a warning that thirty-one people are at risk and the foot crossing must stay open. The inspector interrupts and orders the crossing sealed. Orrin saw him cut the return wire; the hounds have its fuse and his punch mark.
- A rail guardian protects a maintenance timetable. Its furnace burns without fuel. The timetable lists towns lost to several kinds of disaster. All arrivals use one handwriting.
- The inspector demands Mira and the receiving copy. He insists the service depart as recorded and will not permit a different arrival. His rule remains “Passengers only. No substitutions.” Defeating him leaves an empty coat and a clicking punch; it does not establish that the enforcer has ceased to exist.
- At the end of Disk A, Rillbridge exists intact across the river in morning light. Mira sees Bram repairing the blue door. The flood has not happened there yet; thirty-one people can still leave. The town’s festival notice establishes its local date on entry.
- Mira joins the journey narratively but is not a combatant. Avoid placing her in combat or making her a weapon.
- The optional salvage contains a maintenance wage envelope in Orrin's father's handwriting. His father died before Orrin could read. The journal records this if the salvage is completed.

## Disk B: A Town Expecting Rain

- The returned town is experiencing the morning of **13 October**, one day before the historical flood. Bram and the festival notice establish this. Preparations run through the night; the schoolhouse scene explicitly advances to before dawn on **14 October**. There is no gameplay countdown. Larkspur’s clock is separate.
- Mira finds her father, Bram, at the blue house immediately on arrival. He is repairing a hinge and has packed her suitcase for tomorrow’s train. He does not remember their separation. He stays with her after Nell speaks to him.
- Tilda, the baker, has committed flour and borrowed tables to the festival. A broken high-water gauge plate persuades her to cancel it and organize the lower street’s evacuation. Timber blocks the overflow and a crack runs below the wall. The party cannot repair the wall in time.
- The carriage door opens for Mira’s ticket and closes against Bram’s foot. It explicitly refuses an additional passenger. Nobody solves this by changing a name or substituting one resident for another.
- Elspeth, a retired teacher, cannot manage the bridge steps. Nell opens the southern schoolhouse gate to enter the courtyard; its open state persists. Nell and Orrin recover a braked maintenance trolley; Bram straps her chair to it, and Mira brings her class register. The residents wait at the bridge.
- The outfall choice is permanent: the orchard channel floods the fruit trees and drains the keeper’s arena; the millrace keeps the orchard bank dry and floods the control apron. Shallow water costs two movement points in combat. Both keep the level foot crossing above the flood.
- Defeating the sluice keeper lets Orrin lower the maintenance crossing. All **thirty-one residents** cross on foot or on carts before the lower quay floods, arriving through Larkspur’s goods yard without boarding the passenger service. Mira remains a narrative companion, never a combatant.
- Ansel and Iona arrange beds and blankets. His first exchange responds to Nell’s Disk A answer. Tilda acknowledges the orchard outcome. The residents remain after Larkspur’s clock passes midnight; Mira and Bram stay together there.
- At **00:17 on Larkspur’s 14 October**, Nell signs for all thirty-one living arrivals. Her last stroke crosses the date. It matches the premature copy; old saves without the opening find the early copy under Ansel’s blotter instead. The two signatures are shown together. `bReceiptMatched` records the proof that a document can arrive before it is written. The people remain safely at Larkspur; no one boards a train to deliver the paper. Its sender remains unknown.
- An optional work sheet has Orrin’s father’s handwriting and records maintenance shifts a week apart between passenger services both dated 14 October. It repeats his instruction to keep the foot crossing unlocked. The envelope discovery changes Orrin’s recognition line, but is not required to inspect the sheet.
- A new dispatch names Cinderbank siding: two carriages of wounded soldiers, no medical staff listed, departure before dawn. A working-crew carbon already bears Nell and Orrin’s names. They follow the premature copies deliberately, and take blankets for the fourteen people whom the service intends to send without care. Mira stays with Bram. This is the incoming Disk C dispatch.

## Disk C: The Last Ambulance Train

Fourteen wounded soldiers occupy two carriages. Orrin pins the frozen brake hose; departure advances only after the player approves it, with no real-time countdown.
- Before leaving Larkspur, Iona lends blankets. If the orchard flooded, Ansel approves Tilda’s flour credit. If the orchard stayed dry, Tilda and Bram bring stored apples across to pay for flour. Mira remains with Bram; Elspeth’s rescue is not undone.
- **Dena**, the ambulance attendant, and **Ivo**, her orderly, are already tending the patients. Nell brings their covered kettle, allowing both of Ivo’s hands to stay on the stretchers.
- The carriage accepts a patient’s stretcher but refuses Dena and her bag in a staged boarding attempt. The order bears the inspector’s punch mark: fourteen passengers, zero attendants. A footpath cannot move patients six miles over ballast. The maintenance crossing from Disk B is not a universal escape.

## Private author foundation

The return railway preserves the last ordinary day of lost communities. The timetable is an act of rescue that became a bureaucratic loop. The inspector is an enforcer rather than the person who created it. A saved town can become real again only if its people can leave their appointed disaster without the registry replacing them with substitutes. Disk B demonstrates one successful escape through the maintenance crossing. The characters do not yet know the full rule or whether it generalizes.

The timetable's original author is Ada Fenwick, a signal engineer whose family was lost at Rillbridge. She constructed the return service and remains inside its maintenance network. Orrin's father was one of the maintenance crew. His work survives because maintenance time and passenger time pass differently. These truths should eventually be revealed through people, objects, and actions, not a final exposition dump.

Do not fix every catastrophe in advance. Each chapter should make a local problem actionable, resolve something the player cares about, and leave consequences visible in previous locations. Avoid endless “there is a bigger mystery” endings with no local resolution.

## Dates

Do not silently equate the present clock with the returned town's calendar. Mira left on the historical 14 October; the tunnel leads into a returned morning before that flood. The return service can connect different moments. Disk B establishes both local dates in dialogue. Future disks must likewise establish their own local calendar and distinguish it from Larkspur’s clock.
