# Borrowed Tomorrow — the whole adventure

This is the agreed direction for the story reboot. Chapters One and Two are playable with their art installed; Chapters Three–Seven are a plan to develop with the user. Future implementation can change routes and supporting characters while preserving the central pursuit, the emperor's defeat, and the separate final journey into the god's domain.

## The promise

Seventeen-year-old Nell wants to leave her provincial railway town and see the sea with her older brother, Tomas. Imperial soldiers take him during Larkspur's festival. A fleck of the crystal he stole catches in Nell's lantern. It hums, and the handle feels strangely cold. With Orrin, a young mechanic, she pursues the prison trains.

The pursuit becomes a rebellion, a rescue that succeeds while the world changes, a struggle to reunite and fight back, an assault on an emperor becoming a god, and finally a journey beyond the mortal world to defeat the god itself. Railways connect a broad fantasy continent: forests, mines, rival kingdoms, ancient temples, a coast and an airship journey. The tone has warmth, humor, frightening discoveries and room to enjoy traveling with the party.

## What the title means

The empire's patron is worshipped as **the Benefactor**. It lent the empire power: abundant harvests, healing and crystal engines. The founding dynasty pledged future generations' days in exchange. Every miracle brought the empire's prosperity forward and enlarged what the god would eventually take back. The present emperor continued drawing on the bargain, telling himself he could find a way out before collection began.

The stopped towns are the first repayments. Their people did not consent to the bargain. Breaking a local collector releases its captives, but does not recover the years already taken or end the god's claim. Stopping the extraction is a real local victory; ending the source of it requires the final chapter.

The god's body beneath the palace is a conduit into its domain, rather than its whole existence. The empire calls the restraints a prison and thinks it has mastered its patron. The Benefactor permitted the arrangement because the conduit makes the empire dependent and gives it access to mortal lives. Begin with imperial worship, an unexplained lantern hum and a damaged sun mural, without divine speech or a boss cutaway; establish the distinction between conduit and source well before the finale.

## The emperor

The emperor lost his family in a civil war. He wants to preserve the last peaceful day before their deaths. Having failed to repay or escape the bargain, he intends to absorb the palace conduit and stop time permanently. He believes no future will mean no further deaths and nothing left for the Benefactor to collect. His paradise replaces living people's choices with the lives he remembers or wishes they had.

He is an active antagonist throughout the adventure. Show his orders, his interventions and the visible cost of his decisions. He offers Nell the morning before the raid, with Ansel alive and Tomas home. Her initial wish to restore her old life makes that offer a genuine temptation.

## Chapter One — Leaving Home

**Goal:** catch the soldiers who took Tomas. **Local antagonist:** Captain Alban.

Let the player meet Tomas at the festival after a short opening scene. He has returned from the capital and is hiding something. Imperial crystal lamps, the festival blessing and the siblings' promised trip to the sea establish the world through ordinary life. Orrin's independent steam engine is an eccentric work in progress.

Alban arrives to arrest Tomas for stealing a crystal from the palace engine. Stationmaster Ansel, Nell's mentor, intervenes and is fatally wounded. The crystal fractures as Tomas tries to keep it from the captain. A pale fleck catches in Nell's lantern glass. A low resonance travels through its handle and gives her the shivers. Neither a voice nor a demonstrated time stop explains it. Tomas is taken during the struggle. Ansel's final action gives her a way out of the square.

Nell fights through the pursuit hounds, finds Orrin in the western workshop and chooses to pursue Tomas. No component-collection errands stand between the decision and the journey. Orrin joins, bringing a steam lance and an engine that can use the old branch.

At Rillbridge, a living village has been stopped by an imperial collector. The party confronts its guard, breaks the collector and wakes the villagers. Captain Alban's signal calls the operation a repayment. The rescued villagers have lost days and some of their strength; that damage is not silently reversed. The sun emblem on the collector matches the festival lamps. The lantern hums more strongly nearby, but the party does not claim to understand its powers or why they can move in the village.

At the mountain pass, Alban blocks pursuit. He has already sent Tomas east in an armored prison coach. Nell and Orrin defeat him, open the route and escape on their own engine. Alban survives and retreats; the chapter's obstacle has genuinely been overcome. The destination is specific: the prison-train exchange at the Iron Coast. End on Nell and Orrin deciding to follow. Keep the god offstage and silent.

**Progression:** short festival exploration → staged raid → solo escape battle → recruit Orrin → frozen village and collector battle → mountain-pass boss → saved departure. One optional workshop fight rewards equipment. Rest and basic supplies are available without errands. Rescued people acknowledge the result on return visits.

## Chapter Two — Prison Trains

**Goal:** find Tomas before the next transfer. **Payoff:** free the prisoners and learn his destination.

The pursuit reaches the Iron Coast exchange and Cinderbank mining settlement. Nell joins a resistance operation because its train raid offers a concrete route to Tomas. Kit, a boy courier Nell’s own age, joins the raid to get the prisoners onto a safe route. He sometimes gets flustered when Nell thanks him; these innocent hints stay unresolved, without a romance arc or payoff. Recruit Celia, a physician educated in the Benefactor’s religion. Introduce Petra, an imperial knight pursuing the fragment, initially a rival with a sincere belief in the empire’s protection.

The raid succeeds: prisoners escape, the army loses the train and the party learns that Tomas was moved to the capital because he discovered how the crystal engines are paid for. Alban attempts to recapture the freed prisoners. The knight witnesses the army sacrifice a settlement to keep the capital supplied, then helps the escape. Give the knight an earned defection, not an instant change of uniform after one conversation.

Petra helps the escape but does not immediately join the party. Her permanent recruitment belongs to Chapter Three, when refusing to surrender the escapees makes her defection explicit. The party obtains a usable way into the capital. The emperor’s signed order puts the capital’s engine supply ahead of the evacuation. His direct appearance belongs to the capital chapter; the god’s religion gains an uncomfortable second meaning through Celia’s rejected patients. Answers establish that the stopped villages are collection, not accidental engine failures.

## Chapter Three — Beneath the Palace

**Goal:** rescue Tomas and disable the extraction. **Payoff:** Tomas comes home alive. **Reversal:** the emperor merges with the conduit.

Petra refuses an order to hand the escapees back and joins the party, accepting pursuit by her former comrades. Infiltrate the capital, interrupt an execution and fight through the palace. Tomas has been sabotaging the machinery from captivity; his work creates the opening the party uses. The rescue and reunion are substantial scenes, not a clue that sends Nell to another prison.

The party disables the extraction machinery. The emperor makes a new decision in response: rather than surrender his miracles and face the Benefactor, he enters its restrained body. He becomes the conduit of its power. The capital tears free of the ground; the party escapes with Tomas as time begins failing across the continent.

Do not negate the rescue or suggest that every action was secretly the villain's plan. Tomas's survival and the freed prisoners matter in the next chapters.

## Chapter Four — The Broken World

**Goal:** reunite the scattered party and find a way to fight back. **Payoff:** an informed, voluntary commitment to save the world.

Nell wakes with Tomas safe and her original objective completed. She must decide what to do with the world left around them. Other companions carry playable stretches: Orrin organizes an escape from a damaged industrial city; the knight protects people from former comrades; the healer searches the original guardians' sanctuary.

Reunite through actions that resolve local dangers. Discover three ancient anchors that stabilize the palace conduit. Breaking them will free regions from the emperor's expanding time arrest and expose his heart. Establish here that the Benefactor exists beyond the conduit, and that its claim can survive the emperor. A mural or vision shows the hollow sun and a path into its domain.

Nell chooses to lead the counterattack. Tomas helps organize the resistance and stays an active supporting character, rather than being kidnapped again.

## Chapter Five — Taking the World Back

**Goal:** sever the anchors and assemble an assault. **Payoff:** liberated regions and an alliance the player helped create.

Acquire an airship. Let the player choose the order of three major adventures, each tied to a companion's unresolved conflict and a different regional danger. Their purposes, bosses and outcomes must differ; avoid three interchangeable temples with differently colored keys.

Orrin's industrial region supplies the alliance and proves transport can survive without divine crystal power. The knight confronts a former commander and persuades imperial troops to protect their own people. The healer exposes the concealed bargain and helps communities surrender the patron's protection without surrendering each other.

Earlier rescues provide crews, intelligence, shelter and allies. Alban's campaign against the party ends decisively here; choose his final fate when his later scenes are authored. The emperor's projections become less human. His offer of Nell's old life arrives after the player has enough experience of the wider world to understand its cost.

## Chapter Six — The Emperor's Last Day

**Goal:** stop the emperor and release the mortal world. **Payoff:** the human antagonist's complete defeat.

Allied forces open the route to the floating capital. Familiar places have become fragments of the emperor's remembered paradise. His dead loved ones repeat conversations while the living inhabitants lose their ability to choose or act. Allow the player to recognize individual people in that process.

The emperor becomes a monstrous divine vessel. The party uses the knowledge and allies earned across the journey to sever its supports and expose its heart. Defeat him in a full climax with his human motive still recognizable. Nell retains her fragment: its final use belongs to Chapter Seven.

The conduit breaks. The capital returns to earth, and the emperor's grip ends. The hollow sun opens above the world. The Benefactor speaks through the broken conduit and demands repayment for every harvest, healing and engine it supplied. Days start being taken from regions the empire never ruled. The emperor's attempt to evade the bargain has brought collection forward, rather than eliminating the lender.

The party already knows this can happen and has prepared a route. Let the player reunite with the supporting cast, finish optional personal business and deliberately choose the final departure. No false credits or surprise replacement villain.

## Chapter Seven — The God Who Lent Tomorrow

**Goal:** enter the Benefactor's domain, recover the stolen days and end its power to collect. **Payoff:** lasting peace, with a future belonging to the people who live it.

Nell's fragment opens the path through the hollow sun. The party travels beyond the mortal world, through landscapes built from days the god has taken: an ocean hanging over a night sky, the ruins of the empire's first miraculous harvest, and streets where different stolen seasons meet. These places reveal the history of the bargain through people and events. They are not a replay of every old dungeon.

Meet people from earlier collected settlements. The party helps them act and move again, making the promised recovery visible before the final battle. The god offers Nell her old life once more, now with the power to make the offer real. It treats people as payment, and sees their rescue as theft.

The original anchors taught the party how the god joins mortal days to its body. In the domain, the companions sever those bonds at their source. Nell returns her fragment to expose the divine heart and loses her borrowed power. Fight the Benefactor itself in multiple forms; the final transformation draws on the damaged sun motif introduced in Chapter One and developed in later chapters. Victory destroys its capacity to claim people's lives. It cannot return through another emperor or renew the bargain.

Release the stolen days and surviving captives. Do not use a reset to erase the adventure: Ansel and people killed by violence remain dead, decisions and relationships remain, and towns still need rebuilding. Divine engines and effortless miracles cease. Ordinary skill, cooperation and the alternatives developed by Orrin's region make a livable future possible.

End at Larkspur station. Tomas is home. Nell finally boards an ordinary steam train with her friends to see the sea. The first morning after their return has no imperial blessing and needs none.

## Why a young protagonist can win

The fragment gives Nell access to the conflict and a limited shelter from stopped time. It does not make her stronger than the god. Experience and equipment develop her combat ability; companions supply skills she lacks; the guardians explain vulnerabilities; freed communities make the assault possible. Their combined actions create the opening for the final strike. Introduce the final solution's ingredients throughout the journey.

## Rules for later chapters

- The immediate objective names a person, place or action the party cares about. Completing it changes the situation and earns the next destination.
- Give each chapter a local victory, a recognizable antagonist and a consequential discovery. A mysterious letter is not sufficient payment for a chapter of errands.
- Stage the major decisions and reversals with actors, camera movement, sound and playable action. Keep dialogue concise and let players advance or skip it.
- Keep active combat parties to three members. A larger traveling cast can participate in scenes, while reserves retain their own levels and equipment.
- Keep the party's ordinary affection, arguments and humor present between crises. Allow exploration and rest; narrative urgency is not a hidden real-time timer.
- Foreshadow through worship, old imagery and unsettling sensory clues; let the god speak only after later discoveries earn that encounter. The emperor and the Benefactor have related goals but distinct agency. Defeating each resolves a different part of the threat.
- Protect the completed rescue of Tomas and every local victory. The finale restores stolen futures without erasing the cost of the journey.
