# The Emperor — the breakfast window

The Emperor’s public concern for hospitals and winter stores remains real. He also misses the ordinary life of his household, and tries to preserve it by depriving living people of their futures. His wife and the rest of the family died in the civil war in the established outline. No present-day resurrection, pardon or new antagonist is added.

- **Chapter Three, `three-rupture`:** after saying he has buried enough people, he smooths his mourning ribbon and remembers his wife opening the breakfast window to hear the bread cart. He moved her chair out of the draught; she moved it back. Petra points out that people still live in that street. He then makes the existing choice to enter the vessel. This is a brief personal admission under pressure, not foreknowledge of the party’s rescue or a divine voice.
