# Characters and knowledge — Chapter One

| Character | Motive and voice | Knowledge at the saved departure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nell | Seventeen; direct, impatient to leave home, protective of Tomas. Grief sometimes interrupts her practical determination. | Saw the arrest, Ansel's death, the stopped village and Alban's defeat. Knows Tomas is at the Iron Coast. Heard the repayment claim and felt the lantern's hum and cold handle. Does not know what the crystal can do, why they could move in Rillbridge, the full bargain or the emperor's plan. |
| Orrin | Young mechanic; tests explanations with his hands, offers support before explanations, makes occasional dry jokes. | Knows what Nell told him about the raid and crystal. Saw the collector taking light, the villagers wake and Alban retreat. His coal engine works without imperial crystals. Does not have a missing-father subplot. |
| Tomas | Nell's brother, twenty-four; affectionate, worried, trying to protect her. | Knows why he stole the palace crystal. What he discovered will be earned in the rescue chapters. Captive at the Iron Coast exchange; not a combatant or a follower. |
| Stationmaster Ansel | Nell's mentor; practical, gently teasing, willing to oppose armed authority for his people. | Dies during the raid. Only his cap and others' memories remain available. |
| Iona | Supply clerk, dry humor and direct kindness. | Witnessed the aftermath; stays with Ansel and keeps the station open. Knows Nell is pursuing Tomas. |
| Captain Alban | Human imperial officer; precise, coercive, convinced ordinary people owe the empire obedience for its protection. | Took Tomas, oversaw the Rillbridge repayment and tried to recover Nell's fragment. Defeated at the pass and withdrew. Knows more about the collection than he tells the party. |
| Tilda | Rillbridge baker; concerned first with her hands, bread and neighbors. | Knows the army brought the collector, Nell and Orrin freed the village, three days passed, and the coach went east. |
| Elspeth | Retired teacher using a wheelchair; precise, independent, still counting people. | Recognizes her new weakness, helps organize the freed village and tells Nell to go. |
| The Benefactor | Worshipped as the empire's patron; its actual nature belongs to the later author plan. | Makes no direct appearance and never speaks in Chapter One. The crystal's powers and origin remain uncertain. |

Tomas and Alban have new matching portraits and actors. Ansel and Iona retain the original `master` and `clerk` art; these identities are not reused. Nell and Orrin retain their existing directional movement frames. Tilda and Elspeth retain their own matching art. Unseen speakers and narration have no portrait.
