# Chapter Five — Taking the World Back

**Approved full-chapter plan, now playable.** The shared airfield, survey ship, three freely ordered regions, Emperor offer and flagship finale are implemented; see [Act I](chapter-five-act-one.md), [Act II](chapter-five-act-two.md), [Act III](chapter-five-act-three.md), [Act IV](chapter-five-act-four.md) and [the continuation](chapter-five-continuation.md). Entry is the unchanged `chapter-five:anchors` coast departure. The dev flagship preview stays isolated; Vite-only canonical starts open the airfield or Act IV aboard the ship.

## Shape and purpose

A shared opening at the western airfield leads to **four acts**. Hammerplain, Dawnwatch and Windmere are Acts I–III, but those numbers do not prescribe their order. All three destinations are available after takeoff; the player may leave an unfinished region and return. Completing all three opens Act IV, Alban's flagship. The chapter ends with the alliance ready to approach the floating capital. The Emperor and the Benefactor remain the separate climaxes of Chapters Six and Seven.

The immediate problem is reaching the capital without feeding its engines. Each anchor supplies both a different obstacle and something the assault needs: independent engines, crews who will protect civilians, and a way to release the remaining supports safely. Destroying an anchor stops further collection locally. It does not return days already taken or resurrect anyone.

Use a compact flight chart with chosen landings, a readable description of each region's problem, and three visible completion marks. Flights are brief transitions and deck scenes; there is no large free-flight overworld. The survey ship is a resting place with Equipment, supplies and short companion conversations. The full roster travels together; the player retains their chosen one-to-three combat formation. A companion whose region is in focus participates in scenes even when in reserve.

## Shared opening — The western airfield

Bram's directions lead to an airfield whose survey crew has been ordered to surrender its ship for collection. The crew is trying to finish loading instruments and food, not waiting to be recruited. Nell helps clear the seizure party while Orrin and the crew release the mooring machinery. The ship already works; do not make its acquisition a hunt for scattered replacement parts.

The crew agrees to fly supplies and survey the failing sky routes. Bram and the railway communities can keep it supplied. Kit knows the courier landings and turns the copied anchor chart into three useful destinations. On the first flight, let Nell see the coast they freed moving below them. This is the reward for Chapter Four before the next crisis begins.

The first chart explains each destination's immediate need and expected reward. There is no correct first choice and no hidden deadline. An airfield return remains available for rest and purchases throughout the chapter.

## Act I — Hammerplain: keep the furnaces working

**Lead:** Orrin. **Problem:** the foundries are being forced to feed an anchor while their workers' homes stop. **Payoff:** working independent engines and the means to supply the assault.

The imperial works superintendent promises to restore the stopped streets if the workers increase the furnace output. A freight lift carries finished engines away while the workers cannot bring their families home. Orrin recognizes capable mechanics who have been denied control of their own plant. Their first request is practical: reopen the manual cooling race so they can shut the anchor down without cooking the foundry.

Play through a busy casting floor, the cooling works and the anchor gantry. Give the party a choice of approach through the freight lift or the cooling walk, with different terrain and a shared outcome. The workers perform the actual changeover while the party holds the gantry. Orrin's coal-engine experience helps them adapt equipment they already own; he does not single-handedly invent a replacement for the world's industry.

The region's boss is a **mobile furnace carrier** commandeered to burn the replacement engines. It changes lanes and leaves hot metal behind, contrasting with the anchored flagship. Defeating it saves the engines. The party then releases the anchor with the workers, and the stopped streets resume. Show people finding one another, a shift whistle sounding again and freight leaving under ordinary power.

The workers contribute propulsion parts, repairs and supply engines. Orrin accepts that making something useful includes teaching other people to maintain it. His reward is seeing a second crew start an engine without him. A regional weapon fitting for Orrin and an optional heat-resistant coat enter the bag for manual fitting.

## Act II — Dawnwatch: orders worth refusing

**Lead:** Petra. **Problem:** the garrison is ordered to abandon the lower town and defend its anchor. **Payoff:** soldiers and air crews choose to protect their neighbors and join the assault.

Petra's former commander calls her refusal at Cinderbank a betrayal that endangered the empire. Some soldiers remember the medical coaches; others only know the official account. Petra cannot recruit them with a speech or receive an easy pardon. She enters the lower town, opens its shelter gate and helps bring residents through while the commander orders the gate sealed.

Make the evacuation a playable route through the barracks and signal wall, with civilians already moving safely between defended sections. Avoid another countdown escort or a repeat of the Chapter Two loading battle. The player chooses which gun position to disable first; the other remains a tactical complication in the next fight. Both are cleared before the anchor can be reached.

The commander is a **human duel-and-formation boss**, fighting beside shield troops and issuing repositioning orders. Petra faces the consequences of having enforced similar orders herself. Winning breaks the commander's control of the signal wall; it does not convert every imperial soldier to the party's cause. The troops who saw the evacuation refuse the renewed order to fire into the lower town. Others lay down their arms and stay behind.

Petra offers the surrendering commander the protection she wanted for the patients. The anchor is released under the town's control. Volunteer crews bring recognition signals, approach charts and escort craft. Petra earns trust through what she has done, without restoring her old commission. A new shield fitting and an optional field coat go to the bag.

## Act III — Windmere: care after the blessing

**Lead:** Celia. **Problem:** the anchor sustains a hospital's real miracles while taking days from the surrounding settlements. **Payoff:** patients remain cared for, the taking stops, and the alliance learns how to disconnect living supports safely.

A healed patient walks across the ward in the arrival scene. Their family is relieved; Celia recognizes the treatment and the oath she once taught. The hospital's keeper knows the complete pledge and has hidden the surrounding villages' losses. Exposing that omission must not make the healing fraudulent or turn frightened patients into fools.

Before cutting power, the party helps the staff establish a practical alternative: open the disused water lift, bring the existing stores up from the lower dispensary and move dependent patients to the ordinary ward. These are one connected route with people working alongside the party, not a shopping list. Earlier rescues supply medicine and volunteers. Dena sends help through the railway network; Tomas stays safe at the refuge.

The keeper refuses the shutdown and releases the **hospice's mirrored ward engine**. Its boss battle redirects announced light attacks between fixed reflectors, rewarding movement and breaking the correct projection rather than repeating the other anchors' fights. The staff finish the transfer while the party disables it. Cutting the anchor stops the local taking; some conditions still require difficult, continuing care. Celia stays for the first ordinary night shift before departing.

The staff contribute medical crews and a tested sequence for releasing a support without draining the lives attached to it. Records corroborate the Chapter Four source diagram, but do not reveal a ready entrance to the god's domain or give the lantern an explained power. Celia receives a medical focus; an optional reflector for Nell rewards exploring the old dispensary. Both are manually equipped.

## Between regions

Each completed act visibly changes its landing, frees its anchor mark on the chart, and brings a contribution aboard. Main-route enemies use fixed Chapter Five variants and authored encounters; visiting a region later must not secretly strengthen its enemies. Give regional equipment different uses so the first region does not dictate a mandatory build. Tune all three for Chapter Four arrivals, with the finale designed for the combined rewards. Any future increase to the level cap needs its own progression work.

Kit uses his courier book to reconnect crews, hospital staff and settlements. One brief moment of getting flustered around Nell is enough; his work holds the alliance together. Returns to the ship offer food, repairs, disagreements and ordinary conversation. Leave room to enjoy the airship.

After two anchors fall, the Emperor projects a restored Larkspur morning across the ship's empty observation cabin. Ansel is working at the station; Tomas is home. Nell recognizes something specific Ansel used to do and stays longer than she meant to. The Emperor offers to keep that day for her if she brings the fragment to the capital. She leaves the cabin and tells the others what she saw. Her refusal develops in Chapter Six; the projection does not restore Ansel or demonstrate that the Emperor can reverse his death.

Alban's fleet appears on the horizon after the first victory and closes its cordon after the second. These are staged developments, never a real-time pursuit timer. After the third, he blocks the assembled alliance's route to the capital. He is now trying to preserve his command through force, rather than merely enforcing an order heard offscreen.

## Act IV — Alban's flagship

**Goal:** break the fleet cordon. **Payoff:** the party breaks Alban’s command, faces the Benefactor’s retaliation and protects Nell after she falls ill.

The regional contributions bring the survey ship within reach: Hammerplain's propulsion holds it steady, Dawnwatch's signals bring the escort through, and Windmere's crews protect those injured in the approach. Kit coordinates the rendezvous. Put familiar supporting people at their jobs; do not turn the earlier rescue into another threat to Tomas.

Fight from the survey ship's deck, looking upward at an enormous armored flagship. Port and starboard batteries each occupy a three-hex base and have separate health. Their announced lanes force movement. Destroying a battery permanently removes its attacks, making the order a useful choice. Boarding marines contest the deck and push heroes toward danger. The armored bridge opens after both batteries fall, then uses an emergency central barrage. Area attacks hit each component once. The machine is anchored, with mechanical motion rather than breathing.

The campaign and dev preview share those three targets, boarding troops, warning shapes, component destruction, native vectors and the dedicated 10/8 theme. The campaign uses the saved formation and resources; the isolated workshop supplies its own presets and earns no progress or loot.

Destroying the bridge ends the battle. Petra confronts Alban on the command deck, where the boarding marines have returned. Before an arrest, a seam of light opens and the unseen Benefactor accuses the party of breaking its anchors. It destroys Alban, his men and the hull. Kit and Celia get Nell across the gangway as her connection to the shard makes her collapse. Do not show the god's body.

Divine hunters attack the survey ship's medical hatch. Nell cannot fight; the player selects and equips the remaining companions for the defense. Afterwards Kit stays beside her and takes Celia's notes for Sera. The ship holds for the capital's outer mooring. Chapter Six offers an optional cure without making Nell's recovery a condition of the main story. See [chapter-six-plan.md](chapter-six-plan.md).
