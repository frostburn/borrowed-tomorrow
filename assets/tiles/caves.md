# Eastern pass cave materials

Generated with the built-in image tool for Chapter One. The full authoring image stays outside git. Only its bounded native tile pages are published. `caves.json` records the four source quadrants, native dimensions, repeat axes and collision rules.

Run `python3 assets/tiles/import-caves.py /path/to/cave-materials.png` with Pillow installed. The source has a two-by-two layout: wall at top left, shadowed solid roof at top right, worn floor at bottom left, cutaway ledge at bottom right. Crops use half the actual image dimensions. Nearest sampling retains the source colors; cyclic edge blending makes repeating material borders meet. Ledge feet fade into the underlying shadowed rock. Existing page boundaries and IDs stay fixed, and the railway importer retains the cave tiles unchanged.

The wall, roof and floor fields are 192 × 192 pixels; the ledge field is 192 × 144. Each becomes ordinary 24 × 24 tiles. The pass uses wall/roof as its blocked surround, floor on its established route, and ledge lips around the lower recess. The captain's tactical floor uses the same cave materials. No character, marker, interaction or checkpoint coordinates change.

## Generation prompt

Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: reusable cave environment material sheet for a dimensional overhead JRPG railway game. Create ONE square 1024x1024 atlas divided into exactly four equal 512x512 panels, arranged 2 columns by 2 rows, no gutters, labels, frames or text. These are independent reusable tiles/materials, NOT a complete scene or map. Style: rich illustrated pixel art with dimensional chunky rock forms, clean clustered edges, broad readable facets and cool slate, muted violet-grey and brown stone; dark crevices, soft mineral highlights from upper left, restrained moss in cracks only. Match classic detailed railway-town RPG art, rounded volumetric forms, NOT flat 8-bit, NOT vector, NOT noisy tiny gravel, NOT photorealism. Each panel will become a 192px native tile field, so make features broad and clear at that scale. TOP LEFT panel: horizontally seamless natural cave wall face viewed from slightly above, vertical layered craggy slate rock, substantial vertical dimension, about 6 to 8 chunky rock columns across the panel; fill panel edge-to-edge with rock, no sky, grass, floor, objects or empty areas. TOP RIGHT panel: seamless dark cave roof/solid rock texture viewed from above, irregular large fractured rock plates, deep cool shadow but still clearly drawn stone, no black void. BOTTOM LEFT panel: seamless walkable cave floor, worn broad grey-brown stone slabs and compact earth, fine fissures and occasional small embedded stones, relatively calm and horizontal to read as floor, no vegetation or objects. BOTTOM RIGHT panel: horizontally seamless cutaway cave ledge section viewed from slightly above: upper 1/5 shows a light flat grey stone lip/floor, below it a rugged vertical exposed stone face fading darker toward the bottom, bottom edge dark broken rock, substantial 3D depth; no vegetation, no sky, no objects. Lighting and rock colors must be shared across all four panels. Keep the panels accurately aligned to the four quadrants so that they can be cropped without art from neighboring panels.
