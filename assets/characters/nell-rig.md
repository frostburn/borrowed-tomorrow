# Nell's reference-based walk

`src/art/nell-actor.ts` uses the shared configurable gait solver with `NELL_PROPORTIONS` from `src/art/rig-proportions.ts`. Her two leg bones are 9 pixels each, hip baseline 18.8 pixels, hip rise 0.8 pixels, feet 65% of the mannequin's geometric dimensions and root travel 24 pixels per cycle. Shoulders sit 18 pixels above the hips and nine pixels either side of centre; upper arms/forearms are 8/7 pixels. Her right elbow bends to carry the native lantern at her waist. The weapon remains attached to the right grip in every view. This replaces the taller mannequin retarget and matches the compact native overalls/boot proportions without shrinking source artwork.

Nell does not use the mannequin's rectangular limb, hand, foot or weapon meshes. Her clothing is built from rounded cross-sections with separate shoulder, upper-arm, elbow, forearm, thigh, calf and ankle contours. Rounded ends overlap at joints. Sleeves and trouser legs taper toward wrists and ankles. Gloves have rounded palms and separate thumbs. Boots have a narrow heel, raised instep, curved sole and a low rounded toe; both contact endpoints use the configured heel/toe pivots. These contours affect only the artwork, not bone lengths or foot contact timing.

The four existing `public/art/nell*.png` idle actors supply independent directional skins. The exporter prepares four 80 x 80 skins in `assets/characters/nell-skin/`, copying source RGB and setting alpha to 0 or 255 at the existing 128 threshold. Both the workshop and exporter use these canonical pixels, avoiding canvas premultiplication rounding at partially transparent edges. Head/cap/face pixels keep their native size and original resting placement, moving together vertically with the hips. Masked braid pixels remain attached to the head; the central jacket/overalls retain the original torso details. Moving upper arms, forearms, thighs, shins and boots are textured from small garment patches. Transparent samples in these solid material patches use the nearest valid garment sample. Sleeve material patches are registered independently for each view; the left view samples the red upper sleeve rather than the dark edge of the shirt. A rounded trouser seat joins the waist to both upper legs. Thigh fabric extends up to the waist in fixed rings and progressively follows the leg bone below the hips; it does not use the capped sleeve-style cylinder. Seat and thighs share one projected native fabric field from a full 14 x 14 trouser patch, avoiding a texture restart at the hip. The seat belongs to the torso layer.

The lantern uses the original per-view native artwork, with a registered source grip and a mask excluding adjacent clothing. Its textured plane attaches at the right grip and rotates around it with the weapon's pitch. Its existing brass frame, glass, flame and handle retain the source's pixel shapes instead of inheriting the placeholder's box geometry. Depth-buffer occlusion hides the far-side tool naturally. No direction is mirrored.

`src/art/layered-sprite.ts` supports nearest-neighbor textured faces alongside solid materials. Texture alpha participates in the depth buffer: transparent head/costume pixels leave farther limbs visible, while the near arm can occlude the torso. Source RGB values are copied without blending or palette reduction. The assembled silhouette receives the same one-pixel outline as the reference.
