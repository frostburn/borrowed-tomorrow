# Supporting-cast artwork

Created with built-in Image Gen. The approved Nell atlas supplies style and proportions; each character's previous portrait and actor supply identity and costume. The committed native PNGs are the production art. Source-sheet filenames and export sizes are recorded in `supporting-cast.json`; source sheets remain in the work chat.

## Shared portrait/actor prompt

Use case: style-transfer. Asset type: transparent JRPG production sheet with TWO cutouts, one portrait and one world actor of ONE character. Input 1 is the approved Nell PARTY ATLAS: STYLE AND WORLD PROPORTIONS REFERENCE ONLY; do NOT draw Nell or borrow her costume. Input 2 is this NPC's OLD portrait and actor: IDENTITY/COSTUME REFERENCE ONLY, replace its more realistic rendering with the party style.
STYLE MUST MATCH THE FIRST REFERENCE closely: compact 2.75-head-tall super-deformed world actor, large readable simplified anime/JRPG eyes, crisp dark warm outlines, discrete late-16-bit/early-PC pixel clusters, warm upper-left highlights, blue-violet shadows. The portrait has the same simplified face and costume as the actor. No photorealism, painted rendering, tiny realistic face, bloom, dithering or antialiased contours.
LAYOUT: vertical sheet. One large HEAD-AND-SHOULDERS portrait centered in the UPPER HALF, with its complete hair and shoulders visible. One complete compact FRONT-FACING world actor centered in the LOWER HALF, feet visible. The entire portrait is ABOVE the horizontal midpoint and the entire actor is BELOW it, with a wide transparent gap at midpoint. ONLY these two depictions; do not copy the four-direction layout of Nell's reference. Pixel clusters should be large enough to survive export to portrait 128x128 and world actor 80x80, actual figure 64 pixels tall. World face must retain clear eyes and a tiny mouth at that size. Same anatomy, costume, face and colors in both cutouts.
BACKGROUND: genuine transparency. No labels, text, frames, panels, scenery, cast shadows, ground shadows, glow, floating specks, extra figures or extra poses.

## Native export

Run `node scripts/import-character-atlas.mjs <asset-id> <source.png>`. The supporting-cast manifest selects a portrait/actor pair or a single enemy actor. Pair exports find the transparent gap between the portrait and actor. Source and native cutouts discard detached alpha fragments; nearest-neighbor sampling copies complete source RGBA pixels. Per-view phases in `sampling-offsets.json` are selected from native face comparisons, with no painted corrections or palette conversion.

Human and large mechanical figures use a 64-pixel height budget within the 80 x 80 canvas. The hound has a 44-pixel height budget and the crow 32; widths are bounded without stretching. Every world actor meets the common row-68 ground guide. Portraits remain 128 x 128. Runtime code renders the committed art at native size.
